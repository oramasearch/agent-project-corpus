# project-agent-sft-corpus

**SFT training data for the main agent** — the one that creates and mutates Pipelines.
Private. Internal-confidential. Generated by `zetta-bridge/modules/agent-project-corpus`
([zetta#986](https://github.com/oramasearch/zetta/issues/986)).

**      22 examples · 14 mutations.**

## It is a tool-calling agent, so these are trajectories

`ask → call → observation → next call decided BY that observation → Pipeline`

Every call after the first carries a `because` naming a previous **observation**. A call
justified by the user's ask is a scripted step, and a scripted trajectory is a lookup with extra
steps. That check rejects most first attempts, which is the point.

**Quality bar:** perturb one fact in the profile and the correct trajectory must change. If it
survives, the example teaches emission from the use-case *name* rather than reading of the data.

## Mutations are half the corpus, deliberately

Changing a Pipeline is the harder half of the product and the easier half to under-represent:
*"add calibration to that"*, *"actually use the last 90 days"*, *"that ran too long"*. A mutation
carries the pipeline **before** — **what must not change is as much of the lesson as what must**,
and one that silently rebuilds has lost the user's work.

## SFT now, RLVR after — and the order is measured

RLVR sharpens what a policy **already sometimes does**; it cannot originate a capability. A
campaign against a policy that never emits a pipeline returns a uniformly zero reward, and a group
with equal rewards has **zero advantage** — GRPO takes a null step and cannot learn however long it
runs. Measured, not assumed
([zetta#859](https://github.com/oramasearch/zetta/issues/859)).

So this corpus has one job: get the policy to *sometimes* build a correct pipeline. The loop takes
it from there — reward rose 0.52 → 1.00 over 16 steps against the deterministic graders.

## Limitations

- **      22 examples.** A first batch, not a corpus to train on.
- **Roughly half of generations are rejected**, now mostly for methodology-field errors rather
  than structural ones. Genuine rejections, but it means the kept examples skew toward whatever the
  model finds easy to get right.
- **Nothing has been trained on this**, so no evidence it teaches what it intends to.
- **Synthetic**, and the observations are written rather than returned by a real node — a
  trajectory here is what a good one *looks* like, not one that was actually executed.
